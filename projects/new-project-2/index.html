Blinkko

Founding product designer for Blinkko, a shoulder-worn social AI companion (hardware + App). Led the App information architecture and UI system that shipped: four tabs (Explore, Home, Friends, Profile), free-app onboarding, City Explore, remote Glow, and hardware overlays for light, volume, bump, and wardrobe.


UI, Product / 2026

Opportunity

Blinkko is a shoulder-worn social AI companion — hardware plus App — for urban young people. The product should turn going out into exploring the city, making a connection, and showing who you are.

I joined as founding product designer in June 2026, owning software design with the software team: user research, product strategy, information architecture, the UI system, interaction, and getting the App onto the hardware for the September launch.

The problem Functions were scattered. Opening the App felt like configuring a device, not meeting a companion you wear in public.
The job Rebuild the App around real use: home as the hub, five daily spines, 96 touchpoints, software and hardware in one loop.

Insight

If other people can read the glow and the touch, the opening stays low-risk — so you keep wearing it, and it keeps sensing. Wear, sense, understand, respond: a visible companion on the shoulder; light, proximity, and motion; place, people, and events; then light, haptics, and the tail.

Research with Gen Z pushed the interface toward large, bold, spoken type — “sup, kai” instead of settings-speak. The first session should introduce a companion, not configure a device. Dark UI keeps color, light, and Glow as the focus, echoing the physical body.

System

From information architecture to a shared component library. After I took over, I folded scattered functions into five spines and a shared component set. The shipped App is four tabs — Explore, Home, Friends, Profile — with a short shift-and-fade between them on black. Home is the hub: Urbanist greeting, HEVC wardrobe pet, and a control menu for light, volume, closet, and tasks. Explore is the City Explore map (posts, events, check-in, field adventure). Friends is chats plus Send Glow and hardware Bump. Profile holds account and optional device settings. Hardware is optional: no ownership gate on software; light, bump, and three-tap check-in appear when a session is live.

Principles

01 · Type is the interface Large, bold, spoken lowercase (“sup, kai”). That came from looking at how Gen Z actually talks — the type is the UI, not decoration on top of it.
02 · Dark base, light as signal Dark UI lets the character, lights, and Glow take focus, echoing the companion on the shoulder.
03 · State stays in sync Light, volume, bump, and Send Glow have a place in the UI because they exist on the hardware. Tap Glow, and the other person’s tail lights up.

Launch

After software was ready I went back to the US with the team for the September 2026 launch, demoing on-site so people could try the App and the creature on the shoulder together. Plenty wanted to wear it and change the light; fewer knew what to play yet — early content still depends on users. After launch I kept a solo-game exploration toward MVP, and still sit with hardware, marketing, and engineering on demand, schedule, and sign-off.

Hardware owns form, sensing, battery, and the light module — a stable input and output. Software translates that limit into what the shoulder and the App each do. Engineering keeps bump, battery, and light state the same thing on both ends.
